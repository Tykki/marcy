The recent COVID-19 pandemic and the current economic recession has put a great deal of strain on Americans alike. The stress of not being able to find a job or stuck working somewhere that decreases someone’s quality of life puts people on edge. Self-quarantining can jeopardize one’s mental state due to the lack of social interaction or force an individual to be around toxic and triggering stimuli. These factors already place additional strain on dysfunctional family units. Black people, for example, are victim to demoralizing mental illnesses which breeds toxicity within families (as all races do in different ways). With the current racial climate, Black people need to stay united as much as possible and provide support when needed. However, with unchecked and unmonitored mental illness, this becomes impossible. Ultimately, this destroys the Black household, poisoning its roots and the surrounding soil. 

Tiffany Williams of the Perryville Counseling Group holds a Bachelor’s degree in Psychology and Women’s Studies from Bradley University and a Masters in Social Work from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign as well as a CPI (Non-Violent Crisis Intervention and TCI (Therapeutic Crisis Intervention) state certifications. She perceives African American’s rejection of mental illness as a by-product of stereotypes and religion as “Needing Jesus.” She continues by stating, “They [African Americans] look down upon it as a weakness instead of something a person can work on with the right coping tools.” The fear of feeling inferior, especially in a society where Black people are targeted and discriminated against, is exacerbated when one has to be vulnerable when talking about PTSD, anxiety, and/or depression. In essence, these issues are often not addressed as it’s seen as necessary for Black people to be strong in order to weather through bouts of prejudice and discrimination, or at least perceived to be to follow cultural norms and fit into the “in group” or the more accepted social circle.

How does this affect Black families? The lack of communication and the adverse to feeling weak and potentially seen as crazy results in them not talking about their emotions. Additionally, this creates an environment where a member or members of the family unit participate in unhealthy coping mechanisms such as drugs, alcohol, and aggression. In the case the parents are suffering from a mental illness, this creates more trauma for their children and teaches them unhealthy communication skills and coping mechanisms, thus potentially continuing the cycle. Williams states “Sweeping things under the rug resonates into depression, anxiety, and self-harm. So when we don’t talk about big issues and we keep it to ourselves, it manifests into something much worse.”

Despite this worrying trend, there’s potential that the Black community’s view on mental illness will shift. Williams believes social media has an intricate part in normalizing mental illness and the discussions of it. Social media allows individuals to connect to others who may be struggling as well as find outlets to address their symptoms. However, it should be noted that social media is NOT a suitable replacement to therapy.

Changing the view of mental health in the Black community doesn’t completely rely on those affected getting help. Individuals who don’t suffer from a mental illness should be more accepting and help spread awareness. Kanye West’s recent twitter binge and his abrupt announcement that he will run for president for the 2020 election shows what William’s described as the Black community’s initial reaction to mental illness: he’s automatically crazy. While his tweets asserting that Kylie Jenner stinks and that his wife, Kim Kardashian, considered not having his first daughter, North West, were amusing to some, it showed that he’s in the midst of a manic phase. Instead of urging Kanye to seek help, many fans and nonfans either ridiculed him or placed complete blame on the Kardashian clan even though Kanye admitted to suffering from bipolar disorder and not taking medication for it in 2019.
