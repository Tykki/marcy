As the number of cases for COVID-19 increases by the day, the reality that large scale events will have to be put on hold or cancelled feels more like a hard slap to the face than a fever dream. Several festivals have been cancelled such as Lollapalooza and Coachella and concerts were postponed until at least 2021 (Shah). One would think sporting events would also follow this trend due to the danger of COVID-19 spreading; however, as it stands, the 2020 NFL season is to precede as normal sans two weeks from its preseason (Breech). In addition to games being held, fans can still attend the games in-person. The cancellation of the 2020 NFL season and the subsequent loss of Superbowl 2021 won’t bother some, but others will feel the effects of missing out on one of America’s most popular pastimes. 

The fascination with football and the large-scale event to celebrate the end of the season, the Superbowl, started in 1974, seven years after the first Superbowl (Fleeger). With the spread of cable television and the introduction of the internet, football and by extension the Superbowl has reached viewership rates that’s only rivaled during tragedies. The desire to watch the Superbowl isn’t solely reliant on the advertisements. The NFL has become synonymous with patriotism due to the nods it had towards the military and war in past Superbowls such as the military flyover in 1968 and the 1815 Battle of New Orleans war enactment in 1970. The cancellation of Superbowl 2021 will harm businesses since they would lose an opportunity to advertise their products during the biggest viewership day of the year, but it will have a greater negative impact on sports and the deposition of the American people.

Sports wise, the players’ careers would be at a standstill and those who are up for the draft might miss their opportunity to reach the professional field. The fans will suffer due to the lack of content and normalcy. American citizens have had a difficult time adhering to the mask mandate under the impression that it’s an infringement of their rights. The loss of the 2020 NFL season and the Superbowl would be a harsher blow because of its association with patriotism and the expression of freedom many citizens like to misappropriate. Without this outlet, fans might lash out to oppose COVID-19 and continue to label it as a hoax, putting more people at risk to contract it. 

Given the fantastical nature of football fans (rioting in the streets because their favorite team lost), if the stadiums are open to the public, people will fill the seats. While the NFL should proceed with the upcoming season to quiet the louder fans and to serve as a source of strength and inspiration for the American people during this difficult time, the 2020 NFL season and the Superbowl should be cancelled. Until the rate of infection is better controlled, all nonessential gatherings should be put on hold. Not only is the upcoming season putting players, trainers, coaches, and fans at risk, it’s putting the individuals who have to man food courts, prepare the field, and clean the stadium at risk. A football game let alone the Superbowl is a large event and it’s impossible to regulate as over 100,000 people attend.
To follow appropriate social distancing regulations, events are now held remotely. To meet in the middle and to eliminate push back, the NFL should reevaluate its decision to pursue the 2020 season and consider other options such as broadcasting the games on television and streaming sites and not allowing people into the stadium.
